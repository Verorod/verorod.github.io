Case study

Survey Redesign

Role
Lead UX Designer responsible for end-to-end survey experience redesign, including content strategy, survey logic, and cross-functional coordination with Product and Engineering.
Date
October 2024–June 2025

Overview

Gleim is a company that makes exam-prep software and materials. The goal of this project was to help the Content Department and course authors understand where candidates needed more support—based on how customers performed on their real certification exams.

The Challenge

The survey experience was straightforward, but the system behind it created friction for both candidates and the teams maintaining it.

Our team wanted richer feedback and more score reports. Meanwhile, users were being asked about exams they had not taken yet, optional fields felt required, and one survey tried to serve certification paths with very different timelines and scoring systems.

We were also relying on an outdated internal tool to collect responses. Product and Content needed a way to learn from survey data without depending on engineering for every update.

Rethinking Our Delivery

Designing for Three Different Paths

Our existing survey collapsed every learner into one path. I mapped the experience around the three moments that actually shaped what a candidate could answer.

01

Completed Exam

This was the most straightforward path: focus on score collection and post-exam feedback while the experience was still fresh.

02

Not Yet Taken

Gather preparation insights and feedback on course content without making candidates feel bad for not sitting for the exam yet.

03

On a Study Break

Understand barriers sensitively and offer “personal reasons” as an option—giving support teams a chance to help without forcing disclosure.

General Demographic Information and Timing

The triggering moment could not be universal. I charted survey timing against the cadence of each certification so the question arrived when users could answer it meaningfully.

Changing Our Delivery Method

We first considered showing the survey in a modal. But the product already used modals for other interventions, which risked notification fatigue. We tested this method with a different survey and saw more finished forms, but overall completion remained low.

Our solution

Revisit the User Journey

We repurposed an existing header that appeared when a learner completed a course. It was persistent without being disruptive—and a previous survey in that location had already shown strong response rates. For CPA and CMA candidates with different exam timelines, we added targeted email campaigns with context-specific copy.

Designing the Survey

My goal was to improve the clarity of optional questions, tailor content to each certification, and establish a theme within our new survey platform. I connected Product’s knowledge gaps with the feedback candidates could realistically provide.

01

Map

Catalog the questions and internal knowledge gaps.

02

Branch

Wireframe all conditional paths and eligibility rules.

03

Validate

Give Engineering and QA a shared logic diagram.

04

Deliver

Build a reusable, on-brand survey system.

Clarity and Design

The survey changed significantly from the original customer survey, which had several branding changes that were out of date.

The platform’s selected values also enabled conditional logic. Only candidates whose certifications issued numerical results saw a score question, eliminating a frequent source of confusion.

“The visual logic made it possible for stakeholders to validate what users would see before every survey path was built.”

Impact

Comparing the same three-month period year over year, score-report collection rose from 30 reports to 500. The redesign also removed an operational bottleneck: non-engineers can now build and deploy surveys independently.
