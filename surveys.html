Overview

The Challenges

Gleim Exam Prep is a company that focuses on preparing Accounting and Aviation candidates to pass their exams so they can go on to earn their certification or pilot's license. This project focused on Accounting, and was initially pitched as a request to increase the response rate as well as update the questions in the current survey to provide the Content Department with direct feedback on where candidates were struggling in the course.

As the team took a closer look we noticed several other items we needed to consider to stick the landing.

User Experience

Users were being served a question about passing their exam in a different survey that was meant to collect feedback midway through the course. Most candidates had not sat for their exam at that point. There was another, older survey sent at the end of the course, but its questions were so general it rarely produced informative comments.

Product Management

Moving quickly on surveys was surprisingly slow since the process required engineering involvement from beginning to end, even for minor edits. The tools we had for logging responses were outdated and provided no data visualization. Reading the results required familiarity and access to the tool, or someone to walk you through it.

Content Department

  • Even if a user did give their feedback in one of the two surveys, there was no easy way for Content to find an individual customer's comment.
  • Users could indicate whether they passed or failed, as well as their score, but could not upload their score report: the official document with detailed exam results. Support representatives were manually requesting these reports, and it wasn't easily accessible to Content.

That translated into the following goals:

Users

Deliver a timely and relevant survey that asks at the right moment in each candidate's journey.

Business

Increase score report acquisition and overall survey response while creating a better process for how we implement and release surveys.

Content

Give other department leadership visibility into our feedback so they are empowered to make adjustments to their own work based on user needs.

Operational

Enable non-engineers to create and deliver surveys without needing engineering involvement every step of the way.

The majority of my work focused on the Users and Business goals with some improvements to Operational and Content.

Ideation

Designing for 3 Personas

To make sure we were improving our timing and questions for the users, I went over customer feedback and identified 3 user journeys, regardless of certification, we needed to consider.

Completed Exam

This was the most straightforward. We just needed to focus on score collection and post-exam feedback.

Not Yet Taken

Sometimes users reschedule their exam by a day, so they don't have their results yet, but they still finished the course and have valuable feedback.

On a Study Break

This user was the most complex because it's hard to predict why or when a user takes a break. We didn't want to exclude them but wanted to be sensitive to any challenges they had while studying.

To view the precise timing you can view the FigJam file I created for the team while we were sorting out the details.

We adapted our delivery method

I knew in-course notification fatigue at this point in their journey was a real risk, if they received it at all. Chances were good they were either done using the course or they moved on to the next exam part/section. Based on what I had learned about the exam journey, it was more likely that they would be checking their email for exam results, but we couldn't assume the outcome. As a result, we decided to focus on email communication that was uplifting and open to all 3 personas.

Emails
In Course Link

One thing we were aware of, however, is that we had a high response rate for the old Customer Survey. The course had been designed to change the calendar icon to a survey link once their exam date had passed. As mentioned previously, the questions weren't very relevant to their exam experience. So I proposed swapping the surveys, and this proved to be where many of our EA and CIA users were going to answer this survey.

Year check-in

Impact

Comparing the same three-month period year over year, score-report collection rose from 30 reports to 500. The redesign also removed an operational bottleneck: non-engineers can now build and deploy surveys independently.
